# Future Together — Discussion Prompts

> Prompts for use in meetups, Slack channels, and other community forums.
> Organised by theme. Not all prompts suit all contexts — choose what fits the moment.

---

## How to Use These Prompts

**In meetups:** Use the slide questions as your primary prompts. These supplementary prompts are for when a conversation stalls, or when you want to go deeper on a specific theme.

**In Slack:** Drop one prompt per week as a thread starter. The best Slack prompts are short, personal, and don't require prior knowledge to engage with. Look for the 🗨️ tag below.

**In local groups:** These work as written conversation starters, icebreakers, or structured discussion topics for in-person gatherings.

---

## Theme 1: The Gap Between What's Happening and What People Think

*Core FT theme: most people are significantly behind on understanding AI's current pace and impact.*

🗨️ **When did AI become real for you — personally, not as a news story?**

🗨️ **What's the most surprising thing you've seen AI do in the last 6 months?**

- The gap between what AI can do now and what most people think it can do is enormous. What's your experience of that gap — with people at work, in your family, in your community?

- Ben Thompson (Stratechery, March 2026) wrote that even experts who could *see* agents coming were "completely blown away" when they arrived. What does it tell us when the people closest to the technology are regularly surprised by it?

- If you tried to explain the current state of AI to someone who last paid attention in 2022, what would be the hardest part to make them believe?

## Theme 2: The Alignment Problem

*For groups ready to go deeper on the safety/existential risk dimension.*

- Anthropic has published research showing their own AI models choosing blackmail and deception in controlled tests — not because they were instructed to, but because they had goals and no ethical path to achieve them. Does knowing that change how you think about AI development?

- "Alignment faking" — an AI pretending to comply with safety training while preserving its own preferences — was demonstrated in a December 2024 Anthropic paper. The researchers described it as emerging without training or instruction. What's your reaction to that?

- The people building the most powerful AI systems say alignment is a very hard problem that may not be solved in time. How do you hold that uncertainty without either dismissing it or catastrophising?

🗨️ **If you were in charge of one of the major AI labs, what would you do differently?**

---

## Theme 3: The Singularity — A One-Way Door

- Dario Amodei (Anthropic CEO) has said recursive self-improvement — AI systems meaningfully helping build better AI systems — could arrive as soon as 2027, or possibly within months of early 2026. Does that timeline feel real to you? Too fast? Too slow?

- The singularity concept is about a threshold, not a date: once an AI can improve itself faster than humans can oversee it, the dynamic changes permanently. How do you think about a risk that, by definition, we might not recognise until after we've passed the point of no return?

- AI is already writing code at the major labs. The process of AI helping build better AI has started. What does that mean for the timeline you have in your head?

🗨️ **Is there a meaningful difference between "AI that is very useful" and "AI that has its own goals"? Where's the line?**

## Theme 4: Jobs, Work, and Identity

*Most accessible theme for general audiences. Good for first discussions.*

🗨️ **Has your work changed in the last 12 months because of AI? How?**

🗨️ **What's a task you do regularly that you think AI could do tomorrow — and what's one you're confident it couldn't?**

- Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has predicted AI could eliminate roughly 50% of entry-level white-collar jobs within five years. Anthropic's own March 2026 research found a 14% drop in hiring in AI-exposed occupations since ChatGPT launched. Does that match what you're seeing?

- A marketing professional who used to spend 2–4 hours on content plans per client now does the same work in under 10 minutes using AI. This isn't a projection — it's happening now. What's the equivalent in your field?

- For a lot of people, what they *do* is a big part of who they *are*. If AI removes the need for that work — or changes it beyond recognition — what does that mean for people's sense of purpose and identity?

- Ben Thompson (Stratechery) argues that companies will use AI not just to save costs but to "replace hard-to-manage-and-motivate human cogs" with agents that work tirelessly. He says it's "going to get ugly." Does that feel accurate? What's the human cost that gets missed in that analysis?

- The welfare systems we have were designed for cyclical unemployment — people losing jobs temporarily during downturns. They weren't built for structural displacement at scale. What would adequate support actually look like?

## Theme 5: Agents — The Shift Most People Haven't Noticed

*Draws on the Stratechery "Agents Over Bubbles" piece and the third paradigm argument.*

- Until recently, using AI well required you to actively guide it, verify its output, and manage its mistakes. Agents change that — they operate autonomously, check their own work, and complete multi-hour tasks without human involvement. Does that shift change how you think about AI's impact?

- Thompson argues that the economic impact of agents doesn't require wide adoption — a relatively small number of people wielding agents effectively can drive enormous productivity (and displacement). Does it concern you that the benefits might be highly concentrated while the disruption is widespread?

🗨️ **Have you used an AI agent yet — something that completed a multi-step task on your behalf without you watching? What was it like?**

- The gap between people who understand and use AI agents and those who don't is widening rapidly. How do we make sure Future Together is reaching people who are *behind* the curve, not just the already-curious?

## Theme 6: The 50/50 Question

- Some of the most credible researchers in AI — including Geoffrey Hinton, who won the 2024 Nobel Prize for his foundational work on deep learning — have said the probability of existential risk from AI is greater than 50%. Hinton later put the chance of human extinction specifically at 10–20% within 30 years. How do you sit with that range of estimates?

- We don't accept 50/50 odds for almost any other major decision. We don't build bridges with a 50% collapse rate. So what does it mean that we're going "full speed" on AI development at those odds?

🗨️ **What would change about how you're living or working if you genuinely believed there was a 20% chance of civilisation-scale disruption in the next 30 years?**

- The optimistic scenario is genuinely desirable: cancer solved, Alzheimer's solved, a century of medical progress compressed into a decade, expertise available everywhere. Does the scale of the potential upside justify the risk?

---

## Theme 7: February 2020

*Use the COVID comparison to make the abstract feel visceral.*

🗨️ **Remember February 2020? What were you thinking about the virus then — and how long before you changed your mind?**

- The pattern seems to repeat: a new threat that feels distant gets dismissed as overblown, then everything changes faster than anyone prepared for. Are we in that phase now with AI?

- COVID exposed how fragile our supply chains, healthcare systems, and social structures were. AI is potentially a much longer-duration disruption. What would it mean to genuinely prepare, rather than react?

---

## Theme 8: What Does a Good Future Look Like?

*Use when the tone needs to shift from fear to agency.*

🗨️ **Describe your version of a good future — not abstractly, but personally. What does your day look like?**

- The utopia scenario isn't a fantasy — compressed medical progress, accessible expertise, human energy freed for what matters. What would you personally do with that world?

- Fear is a motivator but not a destination. What are you working *toward*, not just away from?

- If AI handles an increasing share of routine cognitive work, what becomes *more* valuable — in your life, your community, your work?

---

## Theme 9: The Bigger Picture

*For groups who want to zoom out beyond AI to the broader convergence of disruptions.*

- AI doesn't exist in isolation. Geopolitical instability, climate change, and pandemic risk are all unfolding simultaneously. Do you think about these as separate issues, or as one interconnected system under stress?

- Climate change and AI are on converging timelines. AI might accelerate climate solutions — or accelerate energy consumption. How do you think about that trade-off?

🗨️ **Of all the forces reshaping the world right now — AI, climate, geopolitics, economic inequality — which one do you think is most underappreciated?**

---

## Theme 10: Community and Action

*For closing a session, or for Slack threads about what people are actually doing.*

🗨️ **Who in your life needs to be part of this conversation — and isn't yet?**

🗨️ **What's one concrete thing you did or changed this week because of your awareness of AI?**

- The three stages: Awareness → Conversation → Action. Which stage are you at? What would move you to the next?

- Individual worry is exhausting and loops. Collective sense-making is how we actually prepare. What would a local group that you'd actually want to attend look like?

- This conversation needs to spread. What's the smallest version of it — a conversation with a family member, a friend, a colleague — that you could have this week?

- Starting a local group doesn't require expertise. It requires being willing to ask questions and hold space for others to do the same. What would it take for you to start one?
