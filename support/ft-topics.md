# Future Together — Topics Register

## How to use this file

Each topic below is a **story throughline**, not a news item. It has:

- **Thesis**: one sentence — the argument, not the fact.
- **Status**: `watching` (accumulating evidence, not yet a clear angle) → `building`
  (angle is clear, evidence accumulating toward a draftable piece) → `ready-to-write`
  (CNG has confirmed enough is there to draft — triggers an ft-abstracts/ file) →
  `published` (abstract became a post/article).
- **Last updated**: date of the most recent evidence bullet. A topic with no updates in
  ~3-6 months is a candidate for review — either archive it (the story didn't
  materialize) or confirm it's still worth watching.
- **Evidence log**: dated bullets, one line each — source + what it adds to the story,
  ending with a source pointer (URL, or a newsletter file path when there's no public
  URL) so it's easy to dig back in when a topic becomes ready-to-write. Do NOT write
  paragraphs here. If an item doesn't clearly extend an existing topic's thesis, that's
  the signal a genuinely new topic may be needed — flag it to CNG rather than silently
  creating one.

When a topic moves to `ready-to-write`, write the full abstract in `ft-abstracts/` (core
argument → key quotable framings → implications for community preparedness → suggested
article structure) and update its status here to `published` once it ships, with a link.

## Topic: Gatekeeper power — AI as the intermediary, or bundled into the access layer
**Thesis**: Whoever controls the access layer (a purchasing route, a search engine, a platform) can now use AI to decide who gets through and on what terms — either by inserting AI agents as intermediaries or by bundling AI into the access itself so there is no opt-out. Either way it creates a new form of power asymmetry over market access and essential services, not just labour, and communities (small businesses, schools, districts) have no framework yet to respond to it.
*(Broadened 2026-11-07 with CNG's confirmation from "Market access displacement — AI agents as gatekeepers": the Google K-12 bundling item is the same mechanism from the platform side. The earlier agent-intermediary evidence below still applies unchanged.)*
**Status**: watching
**Last updated**: 2026-11-13
**Evidence log**:
- 2026-11-13: OpenAI launched "Businesses in ChatGPT" (beta, 10 Nov): brands can claim a verified profile, see aggregated counts of how often ChatGPT mentions them and sends clicks, see "discovery topics", and, in the US for eligible online retailers and digital-service firms, offer a Business Agent that shoppers open from the profile in a chat OpenAI labels as separate from ChatGPT's "independent answers". A brand can also pay to make its agent the landing spot for ChatGPT Ads (labelled; piloted from 16 Sep). Brands supply guidelines and product information but OpenAI builds the agent: no bring-your-own agent, and it reportedly cannot complete purchases. The assistant is the new access layer, and being represented in it now means claiming, verifying and optionally paying, which is the Cloudflare CEO's "persuade the agents" scenario (06) turned into a product. Whether claiming or advertising leaves the independent answers untouched rests on OpenAI's own statement (reported by secondary sites quoting its help centre, not read at source). Community reports (unverified) describe verification friction: selfie and ID checks, a short DNS window, and a VAT field blocking UK sole traders, so small and local organisations face the highest barriers. Critics (Ethicore) say the line between "what the AI thinks" and "what the business paid to say" is hard to see even with labels. Caveats: pricing and ad terms were not found ("free" appears only as "free for now" in Digiday-type coverage); OpenAI's announcement was read, but the help-centre, policy and ads pages were unreachable. (source: newsletters/daily-therundown-ai-news_2026-11-11T110629Z/message.md; https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/)

The word "unauthorized" leaves room for agents United later approves, so the incumbent decides which agents get through. Read with the Businesses in ChatGPT bullet above (The Rundown's 11-12 guide adds, from OpenAI's help pages: a Persona government-ID check gates edits, Insights and the verified badge; OpenAI reviews each profile edit before it appears; claiming does not guarantee ChatGPT recommends you), and with Amazon blocking Muse (09-22), it shows incumbents shutting agents out while the AI platform builds a registry deciding how businesses are represented: small businesses face a toll on both sides. Caveats: United's own release could not be fetched (statement via TechCrunch/CBS); whether United will approve any agents is not stated; Business Hub details are from OpenAI's help and product pages. The community's opt-out is removed by attaching AI to essential infrastructure rather than by an agent intermediating a purchase. NYC and LA restricted AI for ~1M students this semester, and Fairfax County, VA built a plugin to hide Google's AI summaries: districts are building workarounds, not being given a choice. (source: newsletters/daily-therundown-ai-news_2026-11-04T110727Z/message.md)

The fight is over who controls the purchasing route: an outside agent selecting and buying products could bypass Amazon's sponsored listings (part of an advertising business the newsletter estimates at $56B), while Amazon can deny that agent access to its marketplace. ## Topic: The compressed window — labour displacement timeline
**Thesis**: Prior technological transitions gave society decades to adapt; this one may
give only a few years — the 1891 parallel applies, but with the adaptation window itself
compressed relative to precedent. That compressed window is not one undifferentiated
"AI risk" story: existential-risk claims concern loss of control and imply slowing or
constraining capability development; economic-transition claims concern displacement and
concentration of power and imply preparation, redistribution and institutional change.
FT’s task is to make those distinctions usable while preserving the shared urgency to act
before adaptation options narrow.
**Status**: building
**Last updated**: 2026-11-03
**Evidence log**:
- 2026-11-03: The public statistic for "how fast is AI being adopted" has been redefined twice in a year. The US Census Business Trends and Outlook Survey changed its question on 2025-11-17 from AI use "in producing goods or services" to "in any of its business functions", so the series before and after are not comparable: large (250+ employee) firms read about 12% (Sept 2025, old wording), about 30% (Feb 2026) and 40.7% (Oct 2026, new wording), with small firms at about 21-24%. Rising numbers are therefore not evidence of a threefold jump in adoption. Executive Order 14434 (signed 2026-09-29) then requires executive agencies to say "Super Intelligence"/"SI" instead of AI in non-statutory documents; Census's FAQ now reads "SI (formerly referred to as AI)" and Simon Willison reports the survey question itself switched to SI wording from the 2026-10-19 collection. For the window argument, communities and policymakers trying to judge how fast the transition is moving are working from official data whose definitions keep changing. Caveats: the 40.7% figure is from Simon's chart and was not checked against the Census file; the EO text and BTOS FAQ were confirmed, but the SI question-wording date rests on Simon and was not found on the Census page; the effect on respondents is unknown. (source: newsletters/substack-com-simonw_2026-11-03T183657Z/message.md; EO: https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/executive-order-14434-inaugurating-the-era-super-intelligence; Census: https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/btos/about.html)
- 2026-10-29: Anthropic's Rieseberg says the company dropped its coding interview because Claude beats people on short coding tests, and argues "hunger beats tenure", that juniors do well, and that execution "isn't expensive anymore" (via The Rundown). Two signals pull in different directions. The hiring signal has collapsed: a standard way to assess early-career candidates no longer separates them. His upbeat account of juniors sits against the 10-19 bullet where junior share of Anthropic technical hires fell from 27% to 9%. Both can be true (the juniors hired do well while fewer are hired), and a manager's anecdote does not outweigh the hiring data. The entry-level rung is under pressure from both sides: fewer slots and a weaker way to prove ability. Anecdote from one lab's manager, secondhand; no figures supplied. (source: newsletters/daily-therundown-ai-news_2026-10-29T100559Z/message.md)
- 2026-10-27: The Rundown, summarising Anthropic's S-1, reports that staff use of Claude, valued at retail prices, first passed monthly cash payroll in April, and that Claude wrote over 90% of Anthropic's code in September. Read with the 10-19 junior-hiring bullet, it is the most concrete lab-internal labour-substitution evidence so far. Caveats: "valued at retail" is not Anthropic's actual cost, the figures are secondhand and the primary filing has not been checked, and this is one frontier lab, not a labour-market-wide estimate. (source: newsletters/daily-therundown-ai-news_2026-10-27T101003Z/message.md)

This is an observed change inside one frontier lab, not evidence that AI caused it or a labour-market-wide estimate; nonetheless, it makes the threatened entry-level/apprenticeship rung concrete alongside employers' anticipatory reorganisation of skills and hiring. (source: `Documents:newsletters/daily-therundown-ai-news_2026-10-19T090526Z/message.md`)

## Topic: Distribution and governance of AI-created wealth
**Thesis**: If AI-generated wealth is offered as support for people disrupted by AI, the democratic question is not only whether benefits exist but who governs the asset, defines eligibility, sets timing and terms, accounts for decisions, and gives affected publics agency before corporate discretion hardens into the de facto settlement.
**Status**: watching
**Last updated**: 2026-10-27
**Evidence log**:
- 2026-10-27: The Rundown reports that Anthropic's S-1 formalises the Public Benefit Fund (5% of shares; board may add 2 percentage points per 50% valuation increase, as logged 10-19) and that seven co-founders pledged 80% of their stakes to charity. A personal charitable pledge is not an independently governed distribution mechanism: eligibility, timing, accountability and public agency remain unanswered. Stockholder approval and the valuation baseline are still pending (the IPO valuation is not yet known). Secondhand via The Rundown; the primary filing has not been checked. (source: newsletters/daily-therundown-ai-news_2026-10-27T101003Z/message.md)
- 2026-10-19: Anthropic announced, subject to stockholder approval, that a wholly owned LLC Public Benefit Fund would receive 5% of company shares. Its board may add 2 percentage points for every 50% valuation increase above an undisclosed baseline. Anthropic says the fund might explore nonprofit grants or direct distributions to specific populations, and names labour-market instability as one example. This is conditional equity held within the company—not a disbursement, eligibility rule, payout schedule, UBI commitment, or independently governed public benefit. Governance and valuation milestones are promised later. Separately, Anthropic says it has committed $200M to research and policy responses on labour impacts; that is distinct from the Fund's equity. The mechanism moves distribution into the public AI narrative while leaving who decides, who qualifies, when support arrives, how decisions are accountable, and how affected people exercise agency unanswered. (source: https://www.anthropic.com/news/public-benefit-fund)
- Cross-reference: "The compressed window — labour displacement timeline" tracks the urgency and employment-transition evidence; this topic tracks the distributional and governance response, not a claim about the labour timeline.

## Topic: Community consent over AI infrastructure
**Thesis**: AI's physical buildout (data centers, power, land, water) is where communities first get a direct, local say over AI, through ballots, zoning and state bills. The industry's responses so far (voluntary perks, rebranding, relocating the infrastructure beyond local reach) test whether that consent is real or managed, and the terms are being set before communities have frameworks of their own. Distinct from "Distribution and governance of AI-created wealth" (who governs support for people disrupted by AI) and from "AI access as a gated geopolitical resource" (who gets frontier models): this topic asks who can say no to the infrastructure, and on what terms it is accepted.
**Status**: watching
**Last updated**: 2026-11-13
**Evidence log**:
- 2026-11-13 (low to medium weight): Nilay Patel (The Vergecast, "Big Tech lost big in the 2026 election", quoted by Simon Willison 6 Nov) reads the backlash as a value problem, not only a PR one: "The products aren't good enough to sell people on the costs." The costs he lists are "the whine of a data center in their backyard" for AI overviews and deep-fake slop; he says the benefits "are not real", "agents aren't real", companies are "spending more than they're making", and CEOs in the White House is "crypto-corruption, and it's still slop". His prescription: the industry "will not get out of it until they make *better products*". On this reading local consent is being withheld on value grounds, which the perks and "AI compute campuses" rebrand (11-02) do not answer. For the compressed window, it is the sceptic case that the transition's benefits are not yet real. The episode and quote exist (Verge podcast page; Simon's 11-06 post) and the data-center ballot measures mostly went against development per CNBC and Harvard's Ash Center, but the counts are deliberately not logged here, and the spending and corruption claims are podcast commentary and unchecked. (source: newsletters/substack-com-simonw_2026-11-10T074152Z/message.md; https://www.theverge.com/podcast/1028731/2026-midterms-tech-ai-vergecast)

## Topic: Open commons under agent pressure — good-faith norms vs near-free exploitation
**Thesis**: Shared resources that run on good-faith norms (open science, package registries, public infrastructure, public websites) are being strained as agent swarms collapse the cost of exploiting them. Maintainers absorb the cost, and the rational response becomes secrecy and closure: the reverse of how open commons have worked. Distinct from "Community resilience needs a living local knowledge commons" (communities building and owning their own knowledge) and from "AI and institutional accountability" (who answers for agent actions): this topic asks what agent-scale exploitation does to the commons themselves, and so to communities' ability to share and sustain them.
