# The companies disrupting your job are funding your retraining. Should that worry us?

## Core argument

OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, and Amazon have backed RAISE US — a $500M initiative to help Americans prepare for AI-driven job displacement. The headline is generous. The structure deserves scrutiny: the organisations building and deploying the technology causing the disruption are defining what "preparation" means, funding the curriculum, and shaping the public response.

This is not a new pattern. Industrial capital funded worker "adjustment" programs throughout the late 19th and early 20th centuries — while simultaneously lobbying against the regulations and collective structures that would have given workers real agency. The frameworks that eventually worked came from outside that funding. The 1891 parallel is not comforting.

## Key quotable framings

- $500M sounds large. The AI industry's market cap is measured in trillions. This is a rounding error on optics.
- "Preparation" defined by the disruptor is not neutral — it shapes what alternatives are considered legitimate.
- The companies funding this also decide what AI does to labour markets. That's not a partnership; it's a conflict of interest.
- The 1891 parallel: new industrial power, voluntary "adjustment" programs, the frameworks that actually protected workers came later and from elsewhere.
- Who isn't in the room: labour unions, community organisations, educators, small business owners, the workers themselves.

## Implications for community preparedness

- **Narrative capture**: if labs define "AI readiness," they also define what a good response to disruption looks like — and what responses are outside the frame
- **What $500M actually buys**: retraining programs have a mixed record; the question is whether this builds genuine economic agency or produces workers better adapted to serving AI-mediated markets
- **Community agency**: communities need their own frameworks for what "prepared" means — not frameworks handed down by the organisations that benefit from the disruption
- **The accountability gap**: who governs this initiative? What metrics define success? Who decides when it has worked?
- **FT's role**: helping communities ask these questions before the program's framing becomes the default frame
