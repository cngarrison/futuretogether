# Accidental Anonymity

## Core argument

The widespread use of AI to polish professional output — resumes, portfolios, GitHub commit messages — is producing a paradoxical outcome: people who use AI to seem more impressive become invisible as individuals. The more AI-perfect the surface, the less the person beneath it is legible. MacWright calls this "accidental anonymity" — nobody intends to erase themselves, but the cumulative effect of AI-mediated self-presentation is the disappearance of authentic human signal at scale.

The mechanism is not deception. It's optimisation. Each individual is making a rational choice to present their best face. The aggregate effect is a professional landscape where nobody's face is visible at all.

## Key quotable framings

- *"They haven't put themselves out there. They haven't said anything true."*
- *"The perfected, generated, prompted resume is generic and impersonal. It tells me nothing about this person, other than that they use particular tools."*
- Accidental anonymity: you didn't choose to hide, but you did.
- The AI-generated portfolio links to AI-generated GitHub projects with purely AI-generated commit messages — a complete surface with no human beneath it.

## Implications for community preparedness

- **Epistemic infrastructure degradation**: authentic signal is eroding not through malice but through optimisation — a subtler and harder-to-resist force
- **The zombie internet applied to professional identity**: AI-generated surfaces with no legible human behind them, proliferating because the tools are universally available
- **Recognition problem**: communities and employers lose the ability to distinguish genuine human contribution — not because anyone is lying, but because everyone is polishing
- **Competitive paradox**: the more people use AI to compete, the more indistinguishable they become from everyone else using AI to compete — the competitive advantage disappears precisely when adoption reaches saturation
- **The value of legible humanity**: in an AI-saturated environment, being distinctively, recognisably yourself becomes a scarce and valuable signal

## Practitioner evidence: Rowan's avatar confession

*Added Jun 2026 — The Rundown AI, Rowan's Corner*

Rowan Cheung (founder, The Rundown AI) ran an AI avatar — his face cloned with HeyGen, his voice cloned with ElevenLabs — as the host of their Instagram account for approximately one year. The account grew from zero to ~200K followers. The team poured real hours into every video: research, scripting, regenerating audio, editing. It was not "slop" in the traditional sense. Then he shut it off and returned on camera himself.

His conclusion, stated as a defended claim: *"The middle has no moat. Authenticity is the moat now."*

**Why this matters for the abstract:** This is the strongest practitioner-level evidence yet for the core argument. It adds a new dimension: even high-effort, high-quality AI-assisted content — content the audience *knew* was AI-generated — loses to authentic human presence over time. The risk is not just individual invisibility but strategic vulnerability: the middle ground (not slop, not fully authentic) is the worst place to be. Rowan quantifies the asymmetry: *"A 5% gain in authenticity is worth 500x down the line."*

**Key additions to the article:**
- The moat framing: authenticity is now a competitive advantage, not just an ethical preference
- The disclosure paradox: the audience knew, they still preferred the real thing once it appeared
- The strategic trap: AI-assisted-but-not-authentic sits in a middle ground with no defensible position — too polished to be human, too human-shaped to be efficiently machine-generated
- Quote: *"On one side, slop farms cranking out junk. On the other, authentic human brands that people actually trust. Everything in between dies."*

## Suggested article structure

1. **Open with the job application observation** — concrete and relatable: a hiring manager sees AI-generated resume → AI-generated portfolio → AI-generated commits → "I don't know anything about this person"
2. **Name the mechanism** — accidental anonymity: not a choice, not deception, an emergent property of universal AI polish
3. **Extend to other domains** — the AI-written pitch, the AI-summarised meeting, the AI-generated community update; everywhere professional self-presentation happens
4. **The saturation paradox** — AI polish was an advantage when rare; at saturation it's a liability because it marks you as indistinguishable
5. **Infrastructure framing** — this isn't about individual bad actors; it's a systemic shift in what "authentic human signal" means and how legible it is
6. **The FT angle** — what communities can do: value the legibly human, build spaces where authentic voice matters more than polished surface, develop new signals for genuine contribution
7. **Close with the window argument** — we are in the brief period where the problem is newly visible but the frameworks haven't solidified; the choices communities make now will shape what authentic participation looks like
