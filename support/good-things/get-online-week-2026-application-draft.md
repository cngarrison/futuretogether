## Delivery

### 1. Describe the event (200 word limit)

Future Together, in partnership with Tumbarumba Public Library (Snowy Valleys Council) and Red Cross RediCommunities, will host a free 90-minute digital skills event at Tumbarumba Public Library on Friday 23 October, 10:30am–12pm.

The event opens with a short talk on staying safe and confident online, including how to recognise AI-generated scams — a growing concern for older Australians. Attendees then rotate through three hands-on small-group stations (13 minutes each) with 1:1 support for those with their own device: (1) navigating the Be Connected learner website and creating an account, (2) recognising phishing and scam messages and using multi-factor authentication, and (3) using apps and websites for community emergency alerts, run with RediCommunities. The event closes with Q&A, key takeaways, and prize draws.

Target audience is Tumbarumba and surrounding region locals aged over 50. We expect 20–30 attendees, reflecting the library's capacity.

Facilitators: Charlie Garrison (Future Together, lead facilitator), Vicki Hulm (Library Coordinator, Snowy Valleys Council), and Peter Makryllos (Regional Program Manager, Red Cross RediCommunities).

### 2. Enjoyable, interactive, engaging (100 word limit)

With only a few library computers, we're running small-group rotating stations rather than one long lecture — attendees learn by doing, not watching. Each station is short (13 minutes), hands-on, and led by a different facilitator, so people move, talk, and try things themselves, on their own phone or laptop where possible. 1:1 support means no one is left behind. We close with an interactive Q&A and prize draws to keep energy high through to the end, and a genuine invitation to keep learning at future library sessions.

### 3. Use of Get Online Week / Be Connected resources (100 word limit)

One rotation station is dedicated entirely to the Be Connected learner website — attendees create an account and find a course relevant to them, with facilitators helping hands-on. We'll display Get Online Week posters and use Good Things' digital skills tipsheets as station handouts. The opening talk and closing summary both point attendees to the Be Connected website for ongoing self-paced learning, and we'll invite attendees to a follow-up in-person Be Connected session at the library to keep the momentum going.

### 4. Marketing strategies (200 word limit)

We'll draw on our existing Tumbarumba event promotion network: posters and flyers displayed around town, a newspaper advertisement in the local paper, and social media posts in the local Facebook community group. The library will promote the event through its own channels and we'll request inclusion in Snowy Valleys Council community newsletters. Red Cross RediCommunities will share the event through their regional channels, reaching people already engaged in community preparedness. We'll also seek support from the Tumbarumba Business Chamber and Rotary Club to spread the word through their networks and members. Framing the event around genuinely useful, practical skills — not just "learn about computers" — and highlighting free entry, hands-on help, and prizes, should encourage attendance from people who might otherwise feel intimidated by digital skills events.

**Yes** — interested in media coverage. (Note: Vicki Hulm has also flagged interest in running follow-up events at two other Snowy Valleys libraries.)
