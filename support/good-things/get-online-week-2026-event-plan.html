Station plans and materials (fallback options)

The three stations are fallback options, not a mandatory rotation. Use them during the hands-on segment if ad-hoc question groups do not form, for anyone whose question does not fit a group, or for attendees who prefer a structured station.

StationOutcome and activityMaterials / facilitation
1. Be ConnectedFind a useful topic or resource and, if they choose, begin account registration on their own device.2 library devices, printed guide, large-text prompt card. Explain accounts are optional; participant enters their own data and can ask for help without sharing it.
2. Safer onlinePractice pausing on a suspicious message; name one password/MFA improvement.Printed examples, password-manager/MFA overview. Never ask anyone to show or say a password, code, bank detail or ID number.
3. Emergency alertsFind official emergency information and save a trusted pathway appropriate to the participant.Demo phone/device, NSW emergency-information resource card. No claims of guaranteed alert delivery; check official channels in an emergency.

Accessible facilitation, setup and AV

  • Use plain English, large print, good contrast, chairs with clear access, quiet repeat-back support and no forced device use.
  • Arrange three clearly labelled fallback stations, a welcome table and morning tea away from cables; provide one low-noise/slow-help option.
  • Confirm Wi-Fi, power, device charging, screen/TV and audio before doors; have printed guides and hotspot/printed fallback.
  • Ask “Would you like help?” before touching a device. Participants retain control of their device and data.

Safety, risks and response

RiskPrevention / response
Trip, medical or evacuation incidentKeep accessways clear; follow library emergency procedures; call 000 if needed; alert venue liaison; record facts after care is arranged.
Distress, shame or overloadNormalise breaks; offer quieter support and a support person; never pressure participation.
Scam/privacy exposureNo passwords, MFA codes, banking, identity documents or remote access. Stop activity, disconnect if appropriate, contact bank/service through known official channels, and seek Scamwatch/IDCARE support as relevant.
Capacity or crowdingCount attendees, keep to 20–30, maintain accessible routes, offer contact details for future support when full.
Device / Wi-Fi failureUse printed screenshots/resources and a buddy/helper; do not troubleshoot by requesting private credentials.

Privacy, photos, evidence and acquittal

Public photos require an affirmative opt-in. Explain that a public/social-media photo is optional and that declining will not affect participation. Use name tags/seat markers only if useful to help the photographer avoid opt-out participants.
  • Remember: at least one acquittal photo showing attendees present must be taken during the event (backs of people/heads acceptable and preferred) — see the reminders in the run sheet at the hands-on segment and the prize draw.
  • Separately explain respectful delivery-evidence/acquittal images: wide/context images of room, materials, activity and numbers, avoiding identifiable faces where possible. Do not describe these as anonymous; take them respectfully and minimise identifying details.
  • Before any photograph, repeat the purpose. Do not photograph screens with personal content, surveys, consent forms, prize slips or sign-in sheets.
  • Store survey totals, receipt copies, attendance count, approved images and incident record in the restricted event folder. Use only the minimum data; restrict access to delivery/acquittal staff; retain financial records for five years as required.
  • Record at least 10 learner surveys, aggregate results, participant numbers, materials used and photo/evidence log for final report/acquittal. Remove contact details from analysis and do not add people to mailing lists without separate permission.
