Future Together held Get Online Together: Digital Skills for Tumbarumba at Tumbarumba Public Library on Friday 23 October. Eight people attended: farmers, retirees and a local business owner, all over 50. Every registered person turned up and everyone stayed to the end. Peter Makryllos from Red Cross RediCommunities presented a 10-minute segment and helped with hands-on support. The library coordinator, Vicki Hulm, supported the event from the library.

The event was promoted through Tumbarumba Public Library's social media channels, and the library also offered to share it with Snowy Valleys Council for its channels. The local Chamber of Commerce shared the event with its membership. We also used posters and a newspaper advertisement, but in a small community most attendees came through word of mouth, so we'll rely more on library and community networks for future events.
With a small group, the session ran as a single conversational group instead of rotating stations. It ran nearly an hour over time because attendees kept asking questions and wanting hands-on help.

Digital skills support

    Strong passwords and password managers: Everyone created a strong password. Three people were helped one-on-one to find and use the password manager on their own phones. Multi-factor authentication was covered briefly.
    Spotting scams: We ran a scam-spotting quiz using Good Things videos and examples.
    AI awareness: We covered what AI is, and one attendee tried an AI tool hands-on.
    Other skills: We showed how to read QR codes, which attendees used to open the feedback survey. We covered choosing a home internet plan and talked through online chatting with grandchildren and children. Individual questions were answered, including a printer problem.
    Be Connected and RediCommunities: We demonstrated the Be Connected learner website and shared RediCommunities emergency apps.
    Resources: We handed out a pack combining Good Things tip sheets with RediCommunities safety information.

Everyone brought their own phone or laptop, which let us teach on the devices they use every day.

Feedback
Feedback was overwhelmingly positive. The most common responses were "will you do this again?" and "we need more events like this." Comments included (recalled by the facilitator; not recorded verbatim):

    A participant was delighted to discover she already had a password manager on her phone and had been using it without knowing.
    On scams: "I need my husband to understand this." Another said, "I wonder if my kids know this."
    One attendee said she had received a scam exactly like our example the week before. She had rung the number, then hung up when they asked for her card details.

The prize draw was for the best strong password. Every attendee created an excellent one, so choosing winners was difficult.

Learnings: Attendance was lower than the 20–30 we hoped for. In a small community, word of mouth worked better than posters and ads, and awareness will likely build over a series of events. Interest in follow-up is strong from attendees and the library coordinator, who has asked about events at other regional libraries. Learner surveys were not collected on the day because the session ran over. Attendees agreed to complete the survey online using the QR code.
