# Facilitator guide — Get Online Together: Digital Skills for Tumbarumba

**Friday 23 October 2026 · 10:30am–12:00pm AEDT**  
**Tumbarumba Public Library, Prince Street, Tumbarumba NSW 2653**  
Audience: local people aged 50+ · Free · capacity 20–30 · wheelchair accessible

## Facilitation principles

- Be warm, unhurried and practical. Say “we can try this together”, not “that’s easy”.
- Invite choice: participants can watch, practise on a demo device, use their own device or take a printed guide.
- Do not assume anyone has an account, email address, smartphone, eyesight/hearing level or previous experience.
- Never request or view a password, MFA code, recovery code, bank detail, identity document or private message.
- Ask before touching a device. Describe each step aloud and offer large-print/slow-paced help.
- Today’s format: **attendees’ own questions are the main event.** The videos and quiz spark questions; the hands-on time answers them.

## 10:30–10:35 — welcome and safety script

> “Good morning, and welcome to **Get Online Together: Digital Skills for Tumbarumba**. I’m Charlie from Future Together. This is part of Get Online Week, run by Good Things Australia. It’s a free, friendly session for trying useful digital skills at your own pace. You can bring out your own device, use a library device, simply watch, or take the guide home — all are good ways to take part.”

> “We’re doing things a little differently today: your questions are the main event. Please grab some paper and a pen. As we watch some short videos and play a quiz together, write down any question that comes to mind — big or small, related to the video or not. There are no silly questions. After the videos, we’ll group similar questions together and spend our hands-on time on exactly what you want to know.”

> “One important safety rule: no helper will ask for your password, a verification code, bank details or identity documents. Keep those private. If a screen contains something personal, you can turn it away or ask for verbal guidance.”

> “AI can make scam messages, voices and pictures look more believable. That does not mean we need to be frightened. It means we use the same calm steps: pause, check using a trusted contact path, and ask someone before acting.”

Briefly point out toilets, exits, morning tea and the quiet/help area. Have helpers hand out paper and pens. Ask: “Is there anything that would make it easier for you to take part today?” If nothing comes to mind for someone, that’s fine: three stations are ready too.

### Photo and evidence script (before any photography)

> “There are two different photo choices. **Public photos are opt-in only** — please tell us if you would like to be included. We may also take respectful delivery-evidence images of the room, materials and activities for reporting; we will minimise identifiable faces where possible, and we’ll mostly photograph from behind. We will not photograph personal screens, surveys, sign-in sheets or prize slips. Please ask if you would like a photo purpose explained or want to avoid being photographed.”

Do not say photos are anonymous. Do not take public-facing photos of anyone who has not opted in.

## 10:35–10:38 — Be Connected intro

**Goal:** Introduce Be Connected as the “home base” for everything today, and give people the QR code (grant requirement).

> “Be Connected is run by the Australian Government’s eSafety Commissioner. It’s completely free, with no catch. It’s a library of short courses, videos, quizzes and tip sheets for everyday digital skills — video calls, photos, online shopping, safety and more. Everything we try today connects back here, at your own pace, whenever you like.”

- Show the QR code (beconnected.esafety.gov.au). Offer help scanning; a helper can hold a phone steady.
- “Scan now if you like, or we’ll remind you at the end.”

**Troubleshooting:** If someone isn’t familiar with QR codes, a helper can type the address or note it on their handout. Accounts are optional.

## 10:38–10:53 — five Good Things Australia videos

**Goal:** Spark questions. Each video is short (about 1–2 minutes). After each, pause briefly (about a minute) so people can write.

For each: switch to the pre-opened tab, play full-screen with **captions on**, then return to the slideshow tab.

1. **How to Talk to AI** (National AI Centre). Asking a chatbot is like giving directions: the clearer you are, the better the answer. A printed tip sheet is available.
2. **Spotting Text Scams** (Be Connected). Two signs: scammers tell you to act fast, and they use tricks to get a reaction. A text marked “Unverified” at the top could be a scammer pretending to be a business.
3. **Getting Connected at Home** (nbn). Lower-speed plans suit social media and shopping; higher-speed plans suit video calls and streaming. Some providers offer discounts for seniors, pensioners and concession card holders — it’s worth asking.
4. **How to Make Strong Passwords** (Aussie Broadband). **Video only; do not run the quiz now.** Make it creative (15+ characters, words meaningful to you), harder to guess, and unique for every account. “There’s a quiz version you can try in hands-on time.”
5. **Make Your Device Easier to Use** (auDA). Accessibility settings (usually a cog icon) for text size, dark mode, captions and text-to-voice; voice assistants can send messages and set alarms.

**Between videos:**
> “Take a moment to write down anything that came up — it doesn’t have to be about the video.”

**Troubleshooting:** If a video won’t play or sound fails, use the tab’s captions or read the key points from this guide, then move on; don’t troubleshoot in front of the room. If time runs over, trim the pauses, not the videos.

**Close:** “Keep those questions coming — we’ll use them shortly.”

## 10:53–11:00 — group quiz: AI in Your Everyday Life

**Goal:** A light, whole-room activity that shows AI is already part of everyday life.

Switch to the pre-opened quiz tab, full-screen.

> “I’ll read each statement and you call out together: true or false! There are about ten of these — have a laugh, there’s no wrong answer until after you guess.”

- Read each statement aloud; let the room call out; reveal; add a sentence of context.
- “Keep writing down anything you’re curious about as we go.”

**Troubleshooting:** If the quiz is slow to load, read the statements from the tab as they appear, or stop at around five statements and move on. Return to the slideshow tab when finished.

**Options**

- **Solo/self-paced on devices:** the **How to Make Strong Passwords** quiz and the **What is AI?** quiz (suggest just 1–2 of its 4 sections to save time). Helpers share the links.
- **Fallback stations** for anyone whose question doesn’t fit a group, or who prefers structure (details below).

Remind anyone creating a Be Connected account that it’s optional and they enter their own details. Do not ask for or view anyone’s password, MFA code, bank details or ID documents.

### Fallback station 1 — Be Connected

**Goal:** Participants locate one useful learning topic and understand that they can choose whether to create an account.

**Open:**
> “Be Connected is a free learning service with short topics for everyday online tasks. Let’s find one thing that would make your life easier.”

**Prompts**
- “Would photos, video calls, online forms, services, shopping or staying safe be most useful today?”
- “Would you like to browse together first, or would you prefer to watch this on the demo device?”
- “If you choose to make an account, read what is requested and enter your own details. You can skip that today and still use the learning.”

**Demonstrate:** opening the site, using a topic/category, choosing a course, and how to return to it. Ask the participant to point/click if comfortable.

**Troubleshooting:** If sign-up/email verification is slow, stop rather than rushing. Write down the site/topic or use the handout. Do not take their device home or retain their email/password.

**Close:** “What is one topic you might come back to? Put it in your guide.”

### Fallback station 2 — scams, passwords and MFA

**Goal:** Participants practise one calm scam response and name one account-safety improvement.

**Open:**
> “Most scams try to hurry us. The safest first action is not to solve it immediately — it is to pause.”

Show a fictional/sanitised example and ask:
- “What makes this message feel urgent or unusual?”
- “Where would you go to check this independently?”
- “Who could you ask before responding?”

**Core script:**
> “A strong password is different for each important account. A passphrase — several memorable words — can be easier to remember. Multi-factor authentication, or MFA, adds another check after the password. A real service may send a code when *you* are signing in, but nobody should ask you to read that code out to them.”

**If someone thinks they have been scammed:** thank them for telling you; do not investigate their account. Say: “Let’s pause. Contact the bank or service using a known official number or its official app, and consider Scamwatch or IDCARE. You are not to blame.” Escalate to the lead facilitator for support.

**Close:** “Choose one small action: update one important password, turn on MFA, or talk to a trusted person.”

### Fallback station 3 — emergency alerts and information

**Goal:** Participants know at least one official route for emergency information and understand the limits of alerts.

**Open:**
> “Alerts can be useful, but they are not the only source of information. In an emergency, use official channels and follow local advice.”

**With Peter / station lead:** demonstrate finding official NSW emergency information and suitable alert/service settings on a demo device. Avoid promising that an app or alert will always reach someone.

**Prompts**
- “Which local hazards are most relevant to you?”
- “Where could you look if the internet or phone service is unreliable?”
- “Who would you contact or check on?”

**Key message:** For immediate danger call **000**. Keep a charged phone/power bank and a paper contact list where practical. Do not require any third-party account; participants decide whether to install or sign into anything.

**Close:** “Write one official source or preparedness step in your guide.”

**Hands-on close (about 11:36):** give a gentle two-minute warning: “In a couple of minutes we’ll come back together — finish what you’re doing, and keep your notes.”

## 11:38–11:50 — regroup, Q&A and takeaways (Segment 3)

Gather everyone. Use the following close-in prompt:
> “What is one thing that felt useful today? What is one question you still have?”

Answer in plain English. If a question requires specialised advice, say so and offer a trusted official referral rather than guessing. If time is short, prioritise closing warmly over covering every remaining question, and offer to follow up.
