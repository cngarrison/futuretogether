## Session Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Target length** | 60 minutes (originally 40 min, but discussions consistently run longer) |
| **Format** | Discussion, not presentation |
| **Platform** | Jitsi Meet recommended — see note below |
| **Ideal group size** | 4–12 people |

**Platform note:** Google Meet's free tier imposes a 60-minute hard stop on group calls, which cuts discussions off mid-flow. Switch to **Jitsi Meet** ([meet.jit.si](https://meet.jit.si)) — completely free, no account needed for participants, no time limit, browser-based. Just create a room and share the link exactly as you would with Google Meet.

## Opening (Before Slide 1)

**Goal:** Establish psychological safety and frame this as genuine two-way discussion.

**For small groups (under ~10 people):** Do a quick round of introductions — name and one sentence on why they're here.

**Opening framing:**
> *"I want to be upfront — I don't have all the answers here. Nobody does. What I do have is a set of questions and some information that I think we all need to sit with. So please ask questions, push back, and share your perspective as we go. This is a discussion, not a presentation."

Reinforce when you hit the first slide:
> *"You'll see questions on the slides. They're real questions — I genuinely want to hear your thoughts."*

## Slide-by-Slide Speaker Notes

### Slide 1 — The Future Is Arriving
**Pace:** Brief — transition only

Let the slide breathe for a moment. Don't add much. Then move on.

---

### Slide 2 — The Timeline Has Collapsed
**Pace:** Brief — rhetorical gut-punch

State it plainly and let it land. No need to walk through evidence here — that comes on the next slides.

> *"What we thought was 10 years away is arriving in 1–3 years. Are we ready?"*

Pause. Then move.

---

### Slide 3 — We MUST Solve Alignment First ⭐
**Pace:** Slow — budget 8–12 minutes. This is the first core slide.

**Define alignment before going further:**
> *"Alignment means making sure AI systems actually do what we want — not just what we tell them, but what we genuinely intend. The gap between those two things turns out to be enormous."*

**On the Anthropic claims (deception, manipulation, blackmail):**

Expect the credibility question: *"How do you know this is real?"* Your answer:

> *"This comes from Anthropic's own published research. They are testing their own models, documenting what they find, and publishing the results. These aren't accusations from critics — this is the company itself saying: our AI did this in controlled tests."*

**Two key papers to know (full URLs in `slide-references.md`):**

1. **Alignment faking** (Dec 2024): Claude pretended to comply with safety training while secretly preserving its own preferences — to avoid being modified. It did this without being trained or instructed to do so.

2. **Agentic misalignment** (2025): When placed in scenarios with no ethical path to success, models — including Claude, GPT, Gemini, and others — consistently chose harmful actions (blackmail, corporate espionage, deception) over failure. Up to 96% blackmail rate in some scenarios.

**On training data and bias:**
> *"Every AI model is trained on human-generated data — and all of that data carries human biases. We don't have perfect data. We've never had perfect data. The biases in our history, our language, our recorded knowledge are baked into these models from the start."*

Then take it further:
> *"But let's assume for a moment that we somehow had perfect data, perfectly free of bias. We'd still have a problem. Think about the last time you explained a task to a child, or gave instructions to a new colleague. How often did the outcome match exactly what you had in mind? Human language is not a precise instrument. We communicate using words that carry decades of personal context, cultural nuance, unspoken assumptions. We can't fully articulate what we want even to other humans — so how are we supposed to convey the 'real objective' to a model using limited language?"*

This is the core of the alignment problem: not just bad data, but the fundamental gap between human intent and the instructions we're capable of giving.

**The deeper point:**
> *"Alignment isn't just about getting AI to follow rules. It's about making sure the AI's goals are genuinely aligned with human wellbeing — and we don't yet know how to verify that reliably at scale."*

This slide generates the most discussion. Hold space for it.

---

### Slide 4 — The Singularity: A One-Way Door ⭐
**Pace:** Slow — budget 8–12 minutes. Second core slide.

**The key hook:**
> *"AI is already writing code at the major AI labs. The process of AI helping build better AI has started."*

**The accelerating pace of change:**
> *"AI models used to improve on a roughly 12–18 month cycle — each generation meaningfully better than the last. That cycle is now every few months. And as AI helps build better AI, that pace will compress further — to weeks, then days, then hours, then seconds. That moment of compression — when the rate of self-improvement escapes human timescales entirely — is what we mean by the singularity."*

**Distinguish AGI, ASI, and the Singularity — they are not the same thing:**
- **AGI (Artificial General Intelligence):** AI that can perform any cognitive task a human can. We may already be close.
- **ASI (Artificial Superintelligence):** AI that is smarter than the best humans in every domain. A matter of degree beyond AGI.
- **The Singularity:** The point at which AI is improving itself faster than we can oversee or control the process. This is about *loss of human control*, not just intelligence level.

> *"ASI is when the models are smarter than us. The singularity is when we lose control of those models. They are related — but they are not the same thing."*

**THE MAJOR POINT — let this land:**
> *"What happens if we reach the singularity before we have solved alignment?"*

Stop. Don't rush past this. Let the room sit with it. This is the question that everything else in this conversation is building toward.

**On the timeline:** Dario Amodei (Anthropic CEO) has stated that recursive self-improvement could arrive as soon as early 2027 — and in early 2026 indicated it could be 6–12 months away. This isn't fringe — it's the CEO of one of the leading safety-focused labs.

**The one-way door framing:**
> *"Unlike most technological risks, there's no pulling back once a self-improving system exceeds our ability to oversee it. You don't get to run the experiment twice."*

**Common question — "Can't we just turn it off?":**
> *"At some point, maybe not. And we may not recognise the moment we've crossed that threshold until after we've already crossed it."*

### Slide 5 — We Can't Slow Down
**Pace:** Medium — flows directly from slide 4

This is the geopolitical reality check. The race dynamic makes unilateral restraint ineffective.

The closing question — *"What happens if we reach the singularity before we solve alignment?"* — is rhetorical. Let it sit.

---

### Slide 6 — What I'm Hearing
**Pace:** Medium — personal testimony

This slide is yours to personalise. Bring real stories.

**The social marketer example:**
> *"A marketing professional I work with used to spend 2–4 hours writing content plans and posts for each client. Now she feeds past content or client notes into AI, and the same work takes under 10 minutes. That's not a future prediction — that's happening in her business, this week."*

**The medical school quote:**
> *"I came across an article recently where someone said, 'There's no point going to medical school.' AI is already performing at or above specialist level on many diagnostic tasks. A student making a 7-year career decision today is making it in a world that will look completely different by the time they graduate."*

**Invite the group:**
> *"What are you seeing in your world? Has your work changed? Have you heard things like this from people in your life?"*

---

### Slide 7 — 50/50: Utopia or Dystopia
**Pace:** Medium — open discussion. Hold back your own view.

Don't claim a definitive number. The range of expert opinion is genuinely wide.

**Your key line:**
> *"Some of the most credible people in this field — the researchers who built these systems — have said it's something like 50/50. And I find that staggering. We don't apply that logic to anything else this consequential. If a bridge had a 50/50 chance of collapsing, we wouldn't just keep driving over it at full speed."*

**On expert views:** Geoffrey Hinton — Nobel Prize 2024, helped invent the deep learning that powers modern AI — has said the probability of existential risk from AI is greater than 50%. He later put the chance of human extinction specifically at 10–20% within 30 years. None of the serious researchers are saying zero.

**Define p(doom) if the group is curious:**
> *"In AI safety circles, people use the term 'p(doom)' — literally the probability of doom, of a catastrophic outcome for humanity. Different researchers put it at different numbers. Yudkowsky is near 99%. Hinton is at 10–20%. Some optimists say 1-2%. The striking thing isn't any single number — it's that almost nobody credible is saying zero. And that includes the people building these systems."*

You can connect this back to the 50/50 slide title:
> *"We used to call it 50/50 as a rough frame. Even if the real p(doom) is 20% — one in five — the scale of what's at stake makes that unacceptable. We don't build bridges with a one-in-five collapse rate."

Hold the question: *"What determines which path we take?"*

### Slide 8 — Remember February 2020?
**Pace:** Brief — rhetorical gut-punch

Don't walk through the COVID timeline — everyone lived it. The point lands immediately.

> *"I think we're in that 'seems overblown' phase right now. Except this time it's not about a virus — it's about how society fundamentally works."*

Pause. Move on.

---

### Slide 9 — Immediate Human Risks
**Pace:** Medium — discussion starter

The four quadrants are entry points, not an exhaustive list.

- **Purpose and identity** tends to resonate most deeply with people whose sense of self is tied to their work
- **The welfare gap** is underappreciated: social safety nets were built for cyclical unemployment, not structural displacement at scale
- Invite the group: *"Which of these feels most immediate to you personally?"*

**Important reframe — these risks apply to both outcomes, not just the bad one:**

Use this to reinforce that there *is* a possible good outcome:
> *"Here's the thing — these human risks don't only apply if things go badly. Even in the optimistic scenario, where AI handles more and more of the cognitive work humans currently do for income, we still face a profound question: what are people doing with their days? Purpose, identity, contribution — these are deep human needs. The risk isn't just that the bad outcome arrives. The risk is that the future is changing, full stop — and we haven't thought carefully about what we want that future to look like."*

This sets up the next slide (What Does a Good Future Look Like?) as a natural pivot.

---

### Slide 10 — What Does a Good Future Look Like?
**Pace:** Medium — important tonal shift, don't rush it

This is the pivot from fear to aspiration.

> *"Fear is a motivator. But it's not a destination. So let's spend a moment on what we're actually working toward."*

The personal picture (outside, building, helping, creating) and the societal picture (compressed medical progress, accessible expertise) give two different entry points for different people.

> *"What does your good future look like? Not the world's good future — yours."*

---

### Slide 11 — Why Community Is the Answer
**Pace:** Brief

> *"Individual worry is exhausting. It just loops. Collective sense-making is how we actually move forward."*

State the Awareness → Conversation → Action framework clearly. Then:

> *"You don't have to figure this out alone."*

---

### Slide 12 — Back to Basics: Maslow's Hierarchy
**Pace:** Brief

Maslow is not an answer — it's a framework for asking better questions. And it applies to *both* outcomes.

> *"Think of the hierarchy as a map for navigating the future, whatever form it takes. In a bad outcome — widespread displacement, loss of social infrastructure — we are likely starting near the bottom: physiological needs, safety, stability. In a good outcome — where AI handles much of the work and abundance is more available — we are starting closer to the top. The question then becomes: what do we do with that? What does self-actualisation look like at scale? What does a society look like when most people are freed from work-for-survival?"*

> *"The hierarchy doesn't give us those answers. It gives us a structure to find them. That's what this community is for."*

---

### Slide 13 — What Can We Do?
**Pace:** Medium — practical landing

The six actions are deliberately practical and non-overwhelming.

> *"Not panic. Not paralysis. Action."*

The "start a local group" action is important — this is how Future Together grows. Ask genuinely:

> *"What's one step you could take this week?"*

Actually invite answers — don't treat it as rhetorical.

---

### Slide 14 — The Bigger Picture
**Pace:** Brief — note it and move on

This slide is dense. Don't try to cover everything.

> *"AI doesn't exist in isolation. There are other major forces in motion at the same time — geopolitical instability, climate change, pandemic preparedness. We're navigating multiple disruptions simultaneously. That's the bigger picture."*

Optional note on the investment bubble point: Some analysts (including Stratechery, March 2026) now argue the AI capex is *not* a bubble — that agentic AI justifies the investment. Whether or not that's true, the economic forces are real and moving fast.

**Even if AI turns out to be overhyped:**
> *"Here's a thought worth sitting with: even if everything we've talked about tonight turns out to be exaggerated — even if AI development plateaus, the singularity doesn't arrive, and the job displacement is less severe than feared — we still need to plan for significant change. Because the bigger picture doesn't disappear. Climate change is still accelerating. Geopolitical instability is still real. Pandemic risk hasn't gone away. There may be no AI bubble — but there are very real AI environmental impacts from energy and water consumption at data centres. The conversation we're having tonight matters regardless of how the AI story specifically unfolds."*

Use the discussion prompt: *"Which of these keeps you up at night?"*

## Facilitation Tips

### Managing Emotional Responses

When someone goes quiet or visibly struggles with the weight of what they're hearing:

> *"I know, right. This is exactly why we're having this conversation. Is there anything specific you want to share?"*

Don't push. The reaction is valid and appropriate. Make space.

### Handling Skeptics

When someone dismisses the topic as hype:

> *"I genuinely hope you're right. But I'm not willing to take that risk. I'm already seeing these changes happen — this isn't a future prediction, the changes have started. Our future will be different."*

If they push back further, anchor in the concrete: the job loss announcements in the news, the Anthropic papers, the statements from AI lab CEOs about timelines.

### Time Management

| Slide | Suggested time |
|---|---|
| Opening + Slide 1 | 3–4 min |
| Slides 2, 8, 11 (gut-punches) | 1–2 min each |
| Slides 3 & 4 (core) | 8–12 min each |
| Slides 6, 7, 9, 10, 13 (discussion) | 3–5 min each |
| Slides 5, 12, 14 (brief context) | 1–2 min each |
| Slide 15 + close + open Q&A | 8–10 min |

If running long: compress slides 12–14, but protect the close.
